Your friend just lost their pet. You want to help, but you're not sure what to say. Maybe you've never had a pet yourself, or maybe you have and you know that no words feel big enough. Either way, you're here because you care. That already matters more than you think.

## What not to say

Let's start with what to avoid. These phrases, while well-intentioned, often cause more pain:

- **"It was just a pet."** — To them, it was family.
- **"You can always get another one."** — They don't want another one. They want the one they lost.
- **"At least they lived a long life."** — Length doesn't reduce depth of love.
- **"I know how you feel."** — Unless you've lost a pet, tread carefully. Even if you have, every loss is different.

These aren't bad intentions. But grief doesn't need logic. It needs space.

## What actually helps

The most powerful things you can do are often the simplest:

- **Say their pet's name.** "I'm so sorry about Luna." Using the name shows you recognize who they lost — not what.
- **Share a memory.** "I remember when Max stole the turkey off the counter." Laughter and tears can coexist.
- **Just show up.** Bring food. Sit with them. You don't need a script. Presence is the point.
- **Check in later.** The first week has plenty of support. Week three? Month two? That's when loneliness settles in.

## Let them grieve their way

Some people want to talk about it constantly. Others go silent. Some create memorials. Others pack everything away. There is no right way to grieve.

Your job isn't to fix it. It's to witness it. To say, "I see your pain, and I'm not going anywhere."

## Watch for warning signs

Most grief, even intense grief, is a natural process. But watch for signs that your friend may need professional help:

- Withdrawing from all social contact for extended periods
- Expressing hopelessness or saying things like "I can't go on"
- Significant changes in eating, sleeping, or daily functioning
- Talking about self-harm or not wanting to be alive

If you notice these signs, gently encourage them to reach out. The **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** (call or text 988) and the **Crisis Text Line** (text HOME to 741741) are available 24/7.

## It's okay if you don't fully understand

You don't have to understand the depth of their bond to respect it. You don't have to have the perfect words. You just have to be willing to sit in the uncomfortable silence of someone else's pain.

That's not nothing. That's everything.
