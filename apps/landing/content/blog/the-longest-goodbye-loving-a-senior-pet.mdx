There's a moment — maybe it's when your dog takes a little longer to stand up, or your cat stops jumping onto the bed — when you realize time is no longer on your side. Your pet is getting older. And so is the goodbye you're not ready for.

## The slow shift

It doesn't happen overnight. One day they're chasing squirrels; the next, they're content watching from the window. Their muzzle turns white. Their naps grow longer. And somewhere in between, you start holding them a little tighter.

Senior pet care isn't just about vet visits and medications. It's about adjusting your life to match theirs — slower walks, softer beds, quieter evenings together.

## Anticipatory grief is real

You don't have to wait for loss to start grieving. Many pet parents experience what psychologists call "anticipatory grief" — mourning a loss before it happens. You might cry looking at them sleeping peacefully. You might take extra photos, knowing each one could be among the last.

This isn't weakness. It's love doing what love does — holding on.

## Making their golden years golden

Every senior pet deserves comfort in their final chapter:

- **Adjust their environment** — Ramps instead of stairs, orthopedic beds, non-slip mats on hard floors
- **Watch for pain signals** — Panting, pacing, reluctance to move, or changes in appetite
- **Keep routines gentle** — Short, easy walks. Familiar toys. The same spot by the window
- **Talk to your vet regularly** — Pain management and quality-of-life assessments help you make informed decisions

## The hardest question

At some point, you may face the question every pet parent dreads: "Is it time?" There's no formula for this. But most veterinarians suggest asking yourself: "Are they having more bad days than good?"

You'll know. You might not want to know, but you will. Trust yourself. You've loved them this long — you know them better than anyone.

## You're not alone in this

Caring for a senior pet can feel isolating. Friends might not understand why you're grieving someone who's still here. But your feelings are valid. The exhaustion, the worry, the bittersweet joy of each new morning together — all of it matters.
