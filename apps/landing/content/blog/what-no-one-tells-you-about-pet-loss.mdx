You might hear people say, "It was just a pet." But you know better. That little creature was your alarm clock, your therapist, your reason to come home. And now the house is too quiet.

## The grief is real

Pet loss grief is a recognized form of bereavement. Researchers have found that the bond between humans and their pets can be as strong — sometimes stronger — than the bond between humans. Losing that bond triggers the same neurological grief response as losing a person.

So no, you're not "overreacting." You're grieving. And that's exactly what you should be doing.

## The five stages don't come in order

You've probably heard of the five stages of grief: denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance. What most people don't tell you is that these stages aren't a neat checklist. You might feel acceptance on Monday and rage on Tuesday. You might cycle through all five in a single afternoon.

That's normal. Grief isn't linear.

## Empty spaces hit the hardest

It's not the big moments that break you — it's the small ones. The food bowl you almost filled out of habit. The spot on the couch that's still warm in your memory. The sound of the door opening to silence instead of paws.

These empty spaces are proof of how full your life was.

## It's okay to not be okay

You don't need to "get over it" on anyone else's timeline. Some people will understand. Others won't. That doesn't change the validity of what you're feeling.

If you need someone to talk to — someone who won't judge, who understands that your pet was family — we're here. Beside is available 24/7, because grief doesn't keep office hours.

## When to seek professional help

While grieving is natural, there are moments when professional support becomes essential:

- You're having trouble eating, sleeping, or functioning at work for more than two weeks
- You're experiencing thoughts of self-harm or suicide
- You feel completely isolated and unable to connect with anyone

If you're in crisis, please call **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or text **HOME to 741741** (Crisis Text Line). You are not alone.
